Sheet-Pan Adobo Chicken Thighs with Mojo (10 Meals)

Sheet-pan boneless thighs in Puerto Rican adobo, with a garlic-citrus mojo stored separately for day-of. Built to pair with arroz con gandules and maduros.

INGREDIENTS
• 5 lb boneless skinless chicken thighs
• 2 tbsp kosher salt (Diamond Crystal; use 1 tbsp if Morton)
• 1/4 cup olive oil
• 3 tbsp jarred minced garlic
• 2 tbsp dried oregano
• 1 tbsp ground cumin
• 2 tsp sazón con achiote y culantro
• 1/4 cup white vinegar
• 1/4 cup lime juice
• 1 tsp black pepper
• 1/2 cup olive oil (for the mojo)
• 2 tbsp jarred minced garlic (for the mojo)
• 1 tsp dried oregano (for the mojo)
• 1/3 cup lime juice (for the mojo)
• 1/4 cup orange juice (for the mojo)
• 1/2 cup fresh cilantro, chopped (for the mojo)

STEPS
1. Trim any thick fat pads from 5 lb boneless skinless chicken thighs and pat every piece thoroughly dry with paper towels. Toss with 2 tbsp kosher salt (Diamond Crystal; use 1 tbsp if Morton) and refrigerate uncovered while you mix the marinade. Salting separately rather than in the marinade lets it penetrate instead of sitting in liquid — this is what keeps the thighs juicy at 175°F. ⏱ 15m
2. Whisk together 1/4 cup olive oil, 3 tbsp jarred minced garlic, 2 tbsp dried oregano, 1 tbsp ground cumin, 2 tsp sazón con achiote y culantro, 1/4 cup white vinegar, 1/4 cup lime juice, and 1 tsp black pepper. Toss with the salted thighs until every piece is coated. Refrigerate 4–8 hours. Do not go past 12 — the vinegar and lime will start to turn the surface of the meat mealy. ⏱ 4h
3. Set two racks in the upper and lower thirds. Slide two empty half-sheet pans in and heat the oven to 450°F with the pans inside, at least 20 minutes. A screaming-hot pan sears the underside on contact instead of letting the thigh sit and weep. ⏱ 20m
4. Lift each thigh out and scrape the excess marinade off against the bowl rim — you want it coated, not dripping. Wet marinade pools on the pan and steams the meat. Unfold each thigh flat, smooth side up, and space them on the hot pans with room between every piece. Roughly 8–9 per pan.
5. Roast 16–20 minutes, swapping the pans top-to-bottom halfway. Start checking at 16 — pull when the thickest thigh reads 168°F. Boneless thighs run fast and the broiler still has work to do. ⏱ 18m
6. Switch to broil and run the top pan 2–3 minutes until the edges char in spots, then swap and repeat. Watch it the whole time. Final temp should be 175–180°F — thighs have enough collagen that this range is juicier than 165°F, not drier. ⏱ 3m
7. While the chicken roasts, warm 1/2 cup olive oil (for the mojo) with 2 tbsp jarred minced garlic (for the mojo) and 1 tsp dried oregano (for the mojo) in a small pan over low heat for 3–4 minutes — just until the garlic smells sweet and stops smelling sharp. Do not let it brown. Off heat, cool 5 minutes, then stir in 1/3 cup lime juice (for the mojo), 1/4 cup orange juice (for the mojo), and 1/2 cup fresh cilantro, chopped (for the mojo). Salt to taste. ⏱ 4m
8. Rest the chicken 10 minutes, then move it to a rack to cool to room temperature before portioning — stacking hot thighs traps steam and softens the crust you just built. Portion the chicken into containers. Keep the mojo in a separate jar. ⏱ 10m

NOTES
**Storage:** chicken and mojo in separate containers. The mojo keeps a full week refrigerated and the oil will solidify — pull it out 10 minutes before eating, or spoon it on while the chicken is still hot from reheating.

**Reheating:** 2 minutes covered in the microwave, then a tablespoon of mojo over the top. Do not reheat with the mojo already on it — you'll cook off the raw-garlic brightness that's the entire point of keeping it separate.

**Yield check:** ~6 oz cooked chicken per container, alongside 1 cup arroz con gandules and 3–4 oz maduros.

**If you want a vegetable:** there's no room on the sheet pans, so do it separately. A raw cabbage-and-lime slaw is the traditional move and needs no cooking — shred it on prep day, dress each container the morning of.
