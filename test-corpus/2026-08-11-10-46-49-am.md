Arroz con Gandules (10-Meal Prep Scale)

Puerto Rican rice with pigeon peas, scaled for meal prep alongside chicken and maduros. Cooked slightly firm to survive reheating.

INGREDIENTS
• 2 tbsp olive oil
• 4 oz slab bacon or country ham, diced small
• 3/4 cup sofrito
• 5 cup water
• 1 1/2 tbsp Better Than Bouillon chicken base
• 2 3/4 tsp sazón con achiote y culantro
• 1/4 cup tomato paste
• 2 tsp dried oregano
• 4 bay leaves
• 1/4 cup pimento-stuffed green olives, sliced
• 3 cup pigeon peas (gandules), two 15-oz cans, drained and rinsed
• 3 1/2 cup parboiled rice
• 3/4 cup fresh cilantro, chopped (divided)
• 2 limes, cut into wedges (for serving)

STEPS
1. Heat a 7–8 quart caldero or Dutch oven over medium. Add 2 tbsp olive oil and 4 oz slab bacon or country ham, diced small, cooking until the fat renders and the edges brown, 5–6 minutes. Add 3/4 cup sofrito and stir constantly until fragrant and the raw garlic smell is gone, about 5 minutes. Do not let the sofrito brown — you want it softened and aromatic, not caramelized. ⏱ 5m 30s
2. Stir in 2 3/4 tsp sazón con achiote y culantro, 1/4 cup tomato paste, and 1 1/2 tbsp Better Than Bouillon chicken base. Cook 1–2 minutes, stirring, until the tomato paste darkens a shade and loses its raw metallic edge. This step is worth the two minutes — untoasted paste tastes flat across a week of leftovers. ⏱ 1m 30s
3. Add 5 cup water, 3 cup pigeon peas (gandules), two 15-oz cans, drained and rinsed, 2 tsp dried oregano, 4 bay leaves, 1/4 cup pimento-stuffed green olives, sliced, and 1/4 cup of the 3/4 cup fresh cilantro, chopped (divided). Bring to a rolling boil. Taste the liquid now — it should be noticeably saltier than something you'd want to drink, because 3.5 cups of unsalted rice is about to absorb all of it. Add more bouillon if it tastes merely pleasant. ⏱ 8m
4. Add 3 1/2 cup parboiled rice. Stir once to submerge the grains and distribute the peas evenly, then leave it alone at a strong simmer, uncovered, until the liquid drops to the level of the rice and the surface looks matte rather than soupy. At this volume expect 8–12 minutes. ⏱ 10m
5. Give it one stir from the bottom to redistribute, then cover tightly and drop to the lowest heat. Steam 30 minutes without lifting the lid — the larger volume needs longer than the original 20–25 minutes, and every peek costs you steam. ⏱ 30m
6. Kill the heat and let it sit covered 10 minutes. Grains should be separate and just shy of fully tender — a little resistance at the center is correct here, since it will finish cooking during reheating. If any grains are chalky white in the middle, sprinkle over 1/4 cup hot water, cover, and give it 5 more minutes. ⏱ 10m
7. Fluff with a fork and pull out the bay leaves. Spread the rice in a thin layer across two sheet pans and cool to room temperature within 30 minutes before it goes into containers. Cooked rice left to cool slowly in a deep pot is the classic Bacillus cereus scenario — spreading it out is the whole fix. ⏱ 30m

NOTES
**Held back for day-of:** the remaining 1/2 cup {0013} and the {0014}. Store the cilantro in a small container with a slightly damp paper towel; scatter it over the reheated plate along with a lime squeeze. This is what keeps day 5 from tasting like day 5.

**Reheating:** sprinkle 1–2 teaspoons of water over the rice, cover, and microwave 2–3 minutes. The steam re-separates the grains. Dry-reheating packed rice is what makes it gummy.

**Pegao:** if you want the crisp bottom layer, raise the heat to medium for the last 2–3 minutes of step 5 and listen for a faint crackle. It won't survive storage well, so it's a cook's-treat rather than something to portion out.

**Plate math:** roughly 1 cup cooked rice, 5–6 oz chicken, 3–4 oz maduros per container.
